# Yash Kumar Portfolio: Design Philosophy

## Purpose

This website is a hiring document for venture teams. It should make a visitor think:

> Yash has a founder's perspective, can operate independently, understands how to evaluate companies, and follows through on important work.

The site is written for investors, founders, and small operating teams who scan quickly and need evidence before they need personality.

## Positioning

The core positioning is:

**Investment Analyst with a founder's perspective and an operator's follow-through.**

The site should connect three strengths:

1. **Research and investment support:** startup evaluation, investment memos, portfolio-founder engagement, market research, and investment-team tools.
2. **Company building:** co-founding Homescanner, customer discovery, product decisions, team leadership, and shipping from ambiguity.
3. **Founder programs and community:** program coordination, founder events, communication, and dependable execution.

Superb Capital is the primary proof point for venture fit. Homescanner and Superb Realty show why Yash understands founders and can execute with them.

## Content Principles

- Lead with evidence before adjectives.
- Show what Yash owned, how he approached it, and what changed as a result.
- Use numbers with context: what was measured, over what period, and what Yash contributed.
- Keep facts, reported outcomes, and personal interpretation distinct.
- Prefer specific verbs such as evaluated, synthesized, re-engaged, built, shipped, led, and negotiated.
- Avoid generic claims such as visionary, passionate, hardworking, founder-obsessed, or highly motivated unless a concrete example immediately supports them.
- Be candid about limits. Credibility improves when an internal or self-reported metric is identified as such.
- Keep the site concise enough to scan, while allowing deeper evidence through case-study detail views.

## Information Hierarchy

The intended reading order is:

1. Who Yash is and why a venture team should care.
2. Proof of investment work at Superb Capital.
3. Proof of company building through Homescanner and Superb Realty.
4. Evidence of founder support and community execution.
5. Compact career history, education, recognition, and direct contact.

Important work should be visible in the first screen or one clear click away. Do not hide the strongest evidence inside a long timeline, an accordion, or a decorative interaction.

## Visual Direction

The visual language is quiet, editorial, and credible:

- Warm off-white backgrounds for reading and dark charcoal sections for emphasis.
- Deep forest green as the single accent for links, status, labels, and calls to action.
- Thin muted borders and small rectangular corners to create structure without making the page feel like a dashboard.
- Large, restrained Inter typography with clear size and weight contrast.
- DM Mono only for small metadata, labels, dates, counts, and section markers.
- The real portrait is used as an honest identity signal, with no artificial hero artwork.
- Generous whitespace and a narrow reading width make the page feel considered and easy to scan.
- Case studies use white surfaces, light borders, and compact metrics rather than oversized marketing cards.

Avoid dark space themes, neon glow, excessive gradients, decorative particles, ornamental shapes, and motion that competes with the work. The design should feel like a thoughtful investment memo translated into a personal site.

## Interaction Principles

- Navigation stays short: Selected work, Experience, About, and a direct contact action.
- Work can be filtered by Company building, Research & investment support, and Community.
- Every case study opens into a readable detail view with context, ownership, actions, and outcome.
- Buttons begin with clear verbs: Explore my work, View resume, Read the story, Start a conversation.
- Links to live work, LinkedIn, GitHub, email, and the resume are always easy to find.
- Keyboard focus must be visible. Mobile navigation must be usable without hover.
- Motion is restrained and reduced when the user prefers reduced motion.
- No interaction should be required to understand the main positioning or the primary evidence.

## Case Study Template

Each future case study should answer four questions:

1. **Context:** What problem or constraint existed?
2. **Ownership:** What did Yash personally identify, decide, build, or coordinate?
3. **Process:** What actions or trade-offs shaped the work?
4. **Outcome:** What changed, and how is that result measured?

The strongest case studies should include a useful artifact when confidentiality permits: a sanitized memo, market map, research brief, product screen, process document, or event operating plan.

## Voice

The voice is direct, observant, and grounded. It should sound like a capable person explaining useful work to a busy partner, not like a personal-brand campaign.

Use first person where it clarifies ownership. Keep sentences short. Remove words that only make the claim sound more impressive. Let the evidence create the impression.

## Non-negotiables

- Superb Capital must remain prominent as Investment Analyst experience.
- The site must clearly show startup evaluation, investment memos, portfolio support, and the pitch-deck analyser when those claims are current and supportable.
- Resume and email must work from the first screen or header.
- The site must remain readable on a small phone and a wide desktop.
- Do not add metrics, testimonials, clients, investment opinions, or credentials without user-provided support.
- Keep copy and resume dates consistent across the site.
